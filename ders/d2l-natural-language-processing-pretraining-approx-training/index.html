Approximate Training

Recall our discussions in (see related section). The main idea of the skip-gram model is using softmax operations to calculate the conditional probability of generating a context word wow_owo​ based on the given center word wcw_cwc​ in (see equation above), whose corresponding logarithmic loss is given by the opposite of (see equation above).

Due to the nature of the softmax operation, since a context word may be anyone in the dictionary V\mathcal{V}V, the opposite of (see equation above) contains the summation of items as many as the entire size of the vocabulary. Consequently, the gradient calculation for the skip-gram model in (see equation above) and that for the continuous bag-of-words model in (see equation above) both contain the summation. Unfortunately, the computational cost for such gradients that sum over a large dictionary (often with hundreds of thousands or millions of words) is huge!

In order to reduce the aforementioned computational complexity, this section will introduce two approximate training methods: negative sampling and hierarchical softmax. Due to the similarity between the skip-gram model and the continuous bag of words model, we will just take the skip-gram model as an example to describe these two approximate training methods.

Anlatım04 / 06

Summary

  • Negative sampling constructs the loss function by considering mutually independent events that involve both positive and negative examples. The computational cost for training is linearly dependent on the number of noise words at each step.
  • Hierarchical softmax constructs the loss function using the path from the root node to the leaf node in the binary tree. The computational cost for training is dependent on the logarithm of the dictionary size at each step.
