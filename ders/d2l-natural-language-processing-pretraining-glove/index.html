Word Embedding with Global Vectors (GloVe)

Word-word co-occurrences within context windows may carry rich semantic information. For example, in a large corpus word "solid" is more likely to co-occur with "ice" than "steam", but word "gas" probably co-occurs with "steam" more frequently than "ice". Besides, global corpus statistics of such co-occurrences can be precomputed: this can lead to more efficient training. To leverage statistical information in the entire corpus for word embedding, let's first revisit the skip-gram model in (see related section), but interpreting it using global corpus statistics such as co-occurrence counts.

Skip-Gram with Global Corpus Statistics

Denoting by qijq_{ij}qij​ the conditional probability P(wj∣wi)P(w_j\mid w_i)P(wj​∣wi​) of word wjw_jwj​ given word wiw_iwi​ in the skip-gram model, we have

where for any index iii vectors vi\mathbf{v}_ivi​ and ui\mathbf{u}_iui​ represent word wiw_iwi​ as the center word and context word, respectively, and V={0,1,…,∣V∣−1}\mathcal{V} = \{0, 1, \ldots, |\mathcal{V}|-1\}V={0,1,…,∣V∣−1} is the index set of the vocabulary.

Consider word wiw_iwi​ that may occur multiple times in the corpus. In the entire corpus, all the context words wherever wiw_iwi​ is taken as their center word form a multiset Ci\mathcal{C}_iCi​ of word indices that allows for multiple instances of the same element. For any element, its number of instances is called its multiplicity. To illustrate with an example, suppose that word wiw_iwi​ occurs twice in the corpus and indices of the context words that take wiw_iwi​ as their center word in the two context windows are k,j,m,kk, j, m, kk,j,m,k and k,l,k,jk, l, k, jk,l,k,j. Thus, multiset Ci={j,j,k,k,k,k,l,m}\mathcal{C}_i = \{j, j, k, k, k, k, l, m\}Ci​={j,j,k,k,k,k,l,m}, where multiplicities of elements j,k,l,mj, k, l, mj,k,l,m are 2, 4, 1, 1, respectively.

Now let's denote the multiplicity of element jjj in multiset Ci\mathcal{C}_iCi​ as xijx_{ij}xij​. This is the global co-occurrence count of word wjw_jwj​ (as the context word) and word wiw_iwi​ (as the center word) in the same context window in the entire corpus. We further denote by xix_ixi​ the number of all the context words in the context windows where wiw_iwi​ occurs as their center word, which is equivalent to ∣Ci∣|\mathcal{C}_i|∣Ci​∣. Letting pijp_{ij}pij​ be the conditional probability xij/xix_{ij}/x_ixij​/xi​ for generating context word wjw_jwj​ given center word wiw_iwi​, (see equation above) can be rewritten as

−∑i∈Vxi∑j∈Vpijlog⁡ qij.-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}} x_i \sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}} p_{ij} \log\,q_{ij}.−∑i∈V​xi​∑j∈V​pij​logqij​. In (see equation above), −∑j∈Vpijlog⁡ qij-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}} p_{ij} \log\,q_{ij}−∑j∈V​pij​logqij​ calculates the cross-entropy of the conditional distribution pijp_{ij}pij​ of global corpus statistics and the conditional distribution qijq_{ij}qij​ of model predictions. This loss is also weighted by xix_ixi​ as explained above. Minimizing the loss function in (see equation above) will allow the predicted conditional distribution to get close to the conditional distribution from the global corpus statistics.

Though being commonly used for measuring the distance between probability distributions, the cross-entropy loss function may not be a good choice here. On the one hand, as we mentioned in (see related section), the cost of properly normalizing qijq_{ij}qij​ results in the sum over the entire vocabulary, which can be computationally expensive. On the other hand, a large number of rare events from a large corpus are often modeled by the cross-entropy loss to be assigned with too much weight.

The GloVe Model

In view of this, the GloVe model makes three changes to the skip-gram model based on squared loss (Pennington et al.):

  1. Use variables pij′=xijp'_{ij}=x_{ij}pij′​=xij​ and qij′=exp⁡(uj⊤vi)q'_{ij}=\exp(\mathbf{u}_j^\top \mathbf{v}_i)qij′​=exp(uj⊤​vi​) that are not probability distributions and take the logarithm of both, so the squared loss term is (log⁡ pij′−log⁡ qij′)2=(uj⊤vi−log⁡ xij)2\left(\log\,p'_{ij} - \log\,q'_{ij}\right)^2 = \left(\mathbf{u}_j^\top \mathbf{v}_i - \log\,x_{ij}\right)^2(logpij′​−logqij′​)2=(uj⊤​vi​−logxij​)2.
  2. Add two scalar model parameters for each word wiw_iwi​: the center word bias bib_ibi​ and the context word bias cic_ici​.
  3. Replace the weight of each loss term with the weight function h(xij)h(x_{ij})h(xij​), where h(x)h(x)h(x) is increasing in the interval of [0,1][0, 1][0,1].

Putting all things together, training GloVe is to minimize the following loss function:

∑i∈V∑j∈Vh(xij)(uj⊤vi+bi+cj−log⁡ xij)2.\sum_{i\in\mathcal{V}} \sum_{j\in\mathcal{V}} h(x_{ij}) \left(\mathbf{u}_j^\top \mathbf{v}_i + b_i + c_j - \log\,x_{ij}\right)^2.∑i∈V​∑j∈V​h(xij​)(uj⊤​vi​+bi​+cj​−logxij​)2. For the weight function, a suggested choice is: h(x)=(x/c)αh(x) = (x/c) ^\alphah(x)=(x/c)α (e.g α=0.75\alpha = 0.75α=0.75) if x<cx < cx<c (e.g., c=100c = 100c=100); otherwise h(x)=1h(x) = 1h(x)=1. In this case, because h(0)=0h(0)=0h(0)=0, the squared loss term for any xij=0x_{ij}=0xij​=0 can be omitted for computational efficiency. For example, when using minibatch stochastic gradient descent for training, at each iteration we randomly sample a minibatch of non-zero xijx_{ij}xij​ to calculate gradients and update the model parameters. Note that these non-zero xijx_{ij}xij​ are precomputed global corpus statistics; thus, the model is called GloVe for Global Vectors.

It should be emphasized that if word wiw_iwi​ appears in the context window of word wjw_jwj​, then vice versa. Therefore, xij=xjix_{ij}=x_{ji}xij​=xji​. Unlike word2vec that fits the asymmetric conditional probability pijp_{ij}pij​, GloVe fits the symmetric log⁡ xij\log \, x_{ij}logxij​. Therefore, the center word vector and the context word vector of any word are mathematically equivalent in the GloVe model. However in practice, owing to different initialization values, the same word may still get different values in these two vectors after training: GloVe sums them up as the output vector.

Anlatım05 / 07

Summary

  • The skip-gram model can be interpreted using global corpus statistics such as word-word co-occurrence counts.
  • The cross-entropy loss may not be a good choice for measuring the difference of two probability distributions, especially for a large corpus. GloVe uses squared loss to fit precomputed global corpus statistics.
  • The center word vector and the context word vector are mathematically equivalent for any word in GloVe.
  • GloVe can be interpreted from the ratio of word-word co-occurrence probabilities.
